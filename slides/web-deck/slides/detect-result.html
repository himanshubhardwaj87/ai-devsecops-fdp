These are the real numbers from the demo project, and you will see them again live. The logs are generated: 120 normal shoppers and three attackers we planted. Nobody told the model what an attack looks like, yet the three attackers get the three highest scores. Read the last column: the pipeline explains each flag by showing which feature is far from typical. That explanation matters as much as the score, because an analyst has to act on it. Do not hide the fourth row. One ordinary client was flagged. That is the cost of behaviour-based detection, and with real data the false alarm rate is the number you must measure. Be clear that synthetic data is easier than production traffic.
